# Growth audit, 2026-10-13: acquisition and retention

A user-acquisition and growth review of ForkBuild 1.3.0 plus everything
merged up to "The village after dark" (2026-10-13). It asks two questions.
What does ForkBuild already do to bring people in and bring them back? And
which features should come next to grow both? It builds on the four
creative-direction audits in this folder and doesn't repeat their findings,
except where one blocks growth.

This is a review, not a decision record. [Pillars.md](../Pillars.md) still
wins. Every recommendation below says which pillar it serves and which
target in "The numbers to reach" it should move, as the third audit's
finding 5 asks. Recommendations that would bend a pillar are listed as
decisions for the owner, not slipped in.

## Verdict

**ForkBuild has an unusually complete growth engine and no fuel in it. The
product side of the loop is mostly built: no-account entry, a link that
carries the build, link previews, embeds, remixing with credit, a weekly
challenge, building and walking together, installable, eight languages.
It is also instrumented. But the loop has never run, because nobody
outside the project has used it: the launch checklist is 0 of 8 and there
is no playtest record. Before launch, the most valuable growth work is
launching. After that, three gaps limit growth: (1) the share link is too
long for the chat apps where builders actually talk, so the viral loop
leaks at its widest point; (2) nothing brings a player back once the tab
is closed; (3) the counters can't see a newcomer drop out before Publish,
and can't see whether anyone returns.**

- Read the README, Pillars, VISION, the launch kit and its channel drafts,

  page that shows what ForkBuild is" (2026-10-08) to "The village after
  dark" (2026-10-13).
- Read the code that each funnel stage runs on: core/VisitorCount.js (every
  counted event), core/LaunchTargets.js, core/LaunchChannel.js,
  core/StarterJob.js, core/BuildChallenge.js, core/BuilderStamps.js,
  core/DeviceNotifications.js, application/publication/sharing/, the
  rendezvous worker's buildPreview.js, the router and the Home, Challenge
  and shared-link views.
- I didn't play the app for this review. The third creative-direction
  audit played the newcomer path a day ago, and its fixes have since
  shipped.
- There is no usage data. GoatCounter has counted only the project's own
  visits so far, so every estimate of impact below is a judgement and
  should be checked against the counters after launch.

## The funnel today

Graded as a growth manager would grade it for a pre-launch, no-account web
game. **A** means best in class, **C** means a real gap.

### Acquisition: how a stranger first hears of it and arrives (B−)

| Asset | State | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Shareable unit | **A−** | A link carries the signed build itself (`#/s/<payload>`), and the worker's `/b/<payload>` gives it a title, author and picture when pasted. |
| Link length | **C** | About 3,700 characters for the 127-brick castle, and up to 12,000. Discord (2,000) and Telegram (4,096) refuse it, per the roadmap's own "Not done". X counts every URL as 23 characters but people distrust very long links; SMS and QR codes choke on them. |
| Embeds and oEmbed | **A** | Turntable iframe, oEmbed discovery, `/e/embed-*` counted. Fits blogs, teachers and wikis. |
| Search (SEO) | **D** | Hash routing on GitHub Pages. Only the home page can be indexed. No page for a build, a challenge week, a builder or a theme, and no sitemap. |
| Store presence | **C+** | itch.io is live. The PWA manifest is ready, but ForkBuild isn't on Google Play (a TWA), the Microsoft Store (PWABuilder) or any web-game portal. |
| Content for short video | **C** | **Save picture** gives a 1200×630 PNG. There is no turntable clip, no timelapse and no walk-through recording. TikTok, Shorts, Reels and Reddit all reward short video. |
| Launch kit | **B−** | Thorough drafts with `?ref=` attribution per channel. Still leads with r/threejs and crypto communities. Its known-issues list says publishing to Nostr needs a NIP-07 extension, which stopped being true when build notes shipped (2026-10-12). |
| Educators | **B** | An honest one-pager. No classroom feature: a teacher can't collect a class's builds in one place. |

### Activation: does a newcomer build something they're proud of in ten minutes? (B)

| Asset | State | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Time to first brick | **A** | No account, no install. **Try it now** opens a house with a job left to do. |
| First job | **C+** | core/StarterJob.js holds one job (`roof`). One click finishes it. The third audit's Release G item 2 (a chain of four jobs ending near ten placed bricks) hasn't been built. That number is also the gate on new networks. |
| Guided first build | **B** | Six steps with a celebration at the end. Step 3, "Drop in a structure", teaches what `/e/publish-placed-…` leaves out. |
| Name, not account | **A** | A guest walks; **Choose a name** is asked only at Publish or **Walk here with me**. |
| Look and feel | **B+** | The warm village shell, textured bricks, music and a dark look all shipped on 2026-10-13. Screenshots are now worth posting. |
| Funnel visibility | **D** | No count for Home seen, Try it now pressed, the first brick placed, each guide step, or the starter job done. You can see who publishes but not where everyone else stopped. |

### Retention: does a player come back next week? (C)

| Asset | State | Note |
|---|---|---|
| A reason to return | **B+** | A new challenge theme every Monday, with twists and remix weeks. This is the best retention mechanic in the product. |
| A way to be reminded | **D** | Device notifications fire only while ForkBuild is open in the background. Once the tab is closed, nothing reaches the player: no push, email, calendar or feed. The installed PWA doesn't help. |
| Payoff on return | **C** | Stamps on Home are a good start, but nothing says what happened while you were away: who remixed your build, who commented, which people you follow published, or what the new theme is. |
| Feedback on your work | **C** | "Remixed N times" and comments exist. There is no one-tap way for a visitor to say they liked a build. For user-generated-content games, getting feedback on your work is the main reason creators come back. |
| Ongoing projects | **C+** | There's no page of your own builds. Pillars itself calls that open work, and "My Worlds" lists Worlds you visited. Players return to projects they can find. |
| Data safety | **B** | Storage persistence is requested, backup reminders, QR copy to another device. Still, clearing the browser loses everything, so data loss will cause churn. |
| Social glue | **B** | Follow, friends, chat, voice, Walk/Build with me. These retain strongly once two friends are in, but nothing gets the second friend in at the moment of delight. |

### Referral and virality: does each player bring the next one? (B)

The loop exists: build → publish → share link → open → **Edit a Copy** →
publish a remix → share. **Build with me** and **Walk here with me** are
direct invites with their own counts. What's weak:

- The invite is offered after Publish, in a notice. It isn't offered at
  the celebration, which is the moment of highest intent.
- A remix opened from a link has no job of its own. Someone who presses
  **Edit a Copy** and changes nothing has no reason to publish, so
  `/e/remix-published` will lag `/e/remix-from-link`.
- The challenge has no "challenge a friend" invite: a link to this week's
  theme that carries your entry as the thing to beat or answer.

### Measurement (B)

Unusually good for a privacy-first app: fixed paths only, no IDs, honouring
GPC and DNT, with targets and a scorecard script. There are three gaps:

1. **No retention signal.** `/e/second-build` is the only "came back"
   measure, and it needs a second publish. You can't tell whether people
   return.
2. **No activation steps before Publish** (see above).
3. **No channel → outcome link.** `/r/<channel>` counts arrivals. Because
   paths can't be joined, you can't tell whether Reddit visitors build more
   than Product Hunt visitors. That's acceptable, but each channel should
   be read in its own week, as the launch kit already says.

## Recommendations

Ordered by how much growth they unlock per day of work, at this stage.
Effort is rough: **S** is about a day, **M** a few days, **L** a week or
more.

### Do first: launch blockers (no new features)

These come before anything below, in line with the third audit:

0a. **Correct the launch kit's known issues** (NIP-07 is no longer needed
    to reach everyone). Drop the Steem and Blurt ticks from the
    checklist. **S.**

0b. **Finish Release G item 2: the starter-job chain** (roof → chimney →
    path → fence or garden, ending near ten placed bricks), and the guide's
    step 3 rewritten as "Add something of your own". This is the
    activation fix with the most leverage: it moves the median-placed
    target, which is also the network gate. **M.**

0c. **Run the five-person playtest** and write it up as
    `docs/playtests/2026-10-NN.md`. Then **set a launch date** and write it
    at the top of the launch kit.

### Tier 1: before the launch posts go out

These fix the leaks a launch spike would otherwise pour through.

**1. Short share links.** *Acquisition and referral. Pillar 2. Moves:
links opened per link made, and copies per link opened.*
A link as long as `/b/<12,000 chars>` can't be pasted into Discord or
Telegram, where kids, builders and game communities talk. This is the
biggest leak in the viral loop.
- Builds published to **Everyone** already exist as signed Nostr build
  notes. A short link can name the note instead of carrying the build:
  `/n/<note id>` on the rendezvous worker. The worker fetches the note from
  the relays the app already reads, checks it the same way `/b/` does, and
  serves the same preview and redirect. Nothing new is stored and no new
  network is added.
- Builds kept to their link keep today's long link, since there's nowhere
  else for the build to live. Optionally, the owner could choose a
  worker-side store with a short ID; that is a privacy decision (see
  "Decisions").
- Count `/e/share-link-short` beside `/e/share-link`.
- **M.**

**2. Activation counters.** *Measurement. Moves nothing by itself, but
it's needed to know which of the targets is stuck.*
Fixed paths, once a page load, under the same rules as every other count:
`/e/home-seen`, `/e/try-it-now`, `/e/first-brick`, `/e/guide-<n>` (each
guide step reached), `/e/starter-job-done` and `/e/publish-dialog`. Add
them to docs/Privacy.md and the scorecard as a funnel with drop-off between
steps. **S.**

**3. Return-visit buckets.** *Measurement for retention.*
The device keeps its own first-visit date (it already keeps far more). On a
later visit it sends `/e/return-d1`, `/e/return-d7` or `/e/return-d30`
once each, the first time it is opened on or after that day. There's still
no ID, and nothing joinable, but there's now a weekly D1, D7 and D30
return rate against `/` for new visitors. The scorecard reads it as a
pillar 3 target. **S.**

**4. A clip, not just a picture.** *Acquisition. Pillars 1 and 2. Moves:
arrivals per channel, and links opened per link made.*
Next to **Save picture**, add **Save a clip**: a 6–10 second turntable
recorded from the existing `BuildTurntable` with `MediaRecorder`, in 9:16
and 1:1, with the build's name and "Remix it on ForkBuild" burned in. Then
add **Build replay**, a timelapse of the build going up brick by brick,
rebuilt from the build's own bricks in placement order. Timelapses are
what building games are known for on short-video platforms, and every clip
a player posts is an advert they made themselves. This also makes the
launch kit's "30-second clip" easy to produce. **M** for the turntable,
**M** for the replay.

**5. The invite at the moment of delight.** *Referral. Pillar 2 and 3.
Moves: friends joined per build link, and links opened per link made.*
The first-build celebration and the post-publish notice should lead with
one line: **"Show a friend: they can walk in it or build on it"**, with
the native share sheet (`navigator.share`) and the short link from item 1.
Add **"Challenge a friend"** to the challenge page: this week's theme plus
your entry, opening on Join. **S.**

### Tier 2: the first month after launch (retention)

**6. "Since you were last here" on Home.** *Retention. Pillars 2 and 3.
Moves: second builds per build published, and the return rate from item
3.*
A short card at the top of Home for a returning player, worked out on the
device from the announcement index and comment history it already syncs:
*"Your Lighthouse was remixed twice. Ana commented on your Mill. Ken, whom
you follow, published a Bridge. This week's theme: a Windmill. Join."*
Every line is a fact, never a score. This is the payoff that makes a
reminder (item 7) worth acting on. **M.**

**7. A way to remind people without accounts.** *Retention. Pillar 2 (the
weekly challenge). Moves: the D7 return rate.*
In order of how little they ask of a player's privacy:
- **Add the challenge to your calendar.** A static `.ics` subscription
  generated from core/BuildChallenge.js, with an event every Monday naming
  the theme and linking to Join. No data leaves the device; the calendar
  app does the reminding. **S.**
- **An RSS or Atom feed** of the weekly themes and the entries found on
  the networks, generated by the Pages workflow. Builders who use feed
  readers, and the itch.io devlog, get it for free. **S.**
- **A ForkBuild account on Nostr, Mastodon and Bluesky** that posts each
  Monday's theme and a few entries. It's a channel the owner runs, not a
  feature. **S.**
- **Web Push (opt-in).** The rendezvous worker holds a push subscription
  together with the public keys the player follows or published as, and
  sends "Your build was remixed" or "New theme: Windmill". This is the
  strongest reminder and the only one that reaches a closed tab on a
  phone. It's also the first time a server would hold anything tied to a
  player, so it's an owner decision. **L.**

**8. A one-tap "I'd live here".** *Retention for creators. Pillar 2.
Moves: second builds per build published.*
A single appreciation on a build's page, in World View and in Explore,
published as a Nostr reaction to the build note with the device-held key.
It is shown on the build as a fact ("12 people would live here") and in
item 6. Creators who get a response build again. This fits "facts about a
build are welcome as credit" as long as it is never summed per person or
used to rank builders; sorting Explore by it is an owner decision. **M.**

**9. My builds.** *Retention. Pillar 3.*
One page listing every build this device made or helped make: draft,
published or remixed by others, each with **Keep building**. Pillars
already lists this as open work. Unfinished projects are a reason to come
back, but only if players can find them. **S–M.**

**10. A remix gets its own job.** *Referral. Pillar 2. Moves: remixes per
build published.*
When **Edit a Copy** opens, a one-line card offers the same kind of job as
item 0b, scaled to the build: "Make it yours: add a garden, a second
storey or a path". Publish's dialog then says "Your remix will appear in
<maker>'s family tree", which is the reason to publish it. **S.**

**11. A weekly recap.** *Retention and acquisition. Pillar 2.*
Every Monday, the challenge page (and the feed from item 7) opens with last
week's village: the entries standing round the plaza, a collage made with
Save picture and an optional **Builder's pick** chosen by a person (credit,
not a ranking). Each week makes something new to post, and it gives
entrants a reason to see each other's builds. **M.**

### Tier 3: months two and three (new channels)

**12. Pages that search engines can index.** *Acquisition.*
The worker already renders `/b/` previews. Extend it to serve static,
indexable pages for each challenge week (`/c/2026-10-12-lighthouse`) and
each build shared to everyone (`/n/<id>` from item 1), with a sitemap.
People search "3D lighthouse build", "build a castle online" and "free
browser building game". Today none of those searches can find a ForkBuild
page. **M–L.**

**13. Stores and web-game portals.** *Acquisition.*
- Google Play through a Trusted Web Activity, and the Microsoft Store
  through PWABuilder. The manifest is already fit for both. **S–M** each.
- Web-game portals (CrazyGames, Poki, Newgrounds) send large audiences of
  browser players. Most require their own SDK and some require ads, which
  conflicts with "no advertising" in Privacy and Educators. An owner
  decision; Newgrounds and itch.io have no such requirement.
- Add each to core/LaunchChannel.js's fixed list (`play`, `msstore`,
  `newgrounds`, `discord`, `youtube`, `tiktok`) so their arrivals are
  counted.

**14. A class space for teachers.** *Acquisition through a channel that
keeps itself going. All three pillars.*
A teacher presses **Start a class** and gets a class link and QR code.
Students who open it build with the class's tag pinned, and the teacher's
page lists every build shared with that class link. The existing tags and
challenge-entry discovery do most of this already. Builds must stay
unannounced to the public by default, so a class works with **Only
people I send the link to**, collected over peer connections or a class
plot. One teacher brings 30 students a term, and the term repeats. Needs a
privacy review before it is offered to schools (Educators.md already says
so). **L.**

**15. Creator links.** *Acquisition.*
Let a builder or YouTuber get a fixed `?ref=c-<slug>` they can put in a
video description (an allow-list kept in core/LaunchChannel.js, added on
request). Their arrivals can then be counted, and the project can thank
the creators who bring people. **S.**

## What not to do

- **Points, streaks, levels, daily login rewards or leaderboards of
  people.** They lift short-term retention in other games, and Pillars
  rules them out. Items 6, 8 and 11 get the same "come back" effect from
  facts about builds instead.
- **Paid acquisition before activation is measured and fixed.** Until the
  activation counters (item 2) show newcomers placing ten bricks, paid
  traffic buys bounces.
- **Launching in crypto communities first.** It brings people who came for
  the chains, not the building, and it sets the wrong first impression for
  press. Lead with builders, cozy-game players and teachers, as the second
  and third audits said.
- **More World View polish before the loop runs.** The world is pretty
  enough to show people now.
- **New networks.** The gate in Pillars stays shut until the building
  targets are met.

## A 90-day plan

| When | Work | Read on the scorecard |
|---|---|---|
| Week 0 | 0a–0c; items 2 and 3 (counters) | The baseline funnel, from the playtest and the first visitors |
| Week 1 | Items 1 (short links), 5 (invite) and 4 (turntable clip) | Links opened per link made |
| Week 2 | Launch: a builders' or cozy-game community with a clip, then Reddit, one community a day | Arrivals per channel; the activation funnel |
| Weeks 3–4 | Items 6, 7 (calendar, feed, social accounts), 9 and 10 | D7 return rate; remixes per build published |
| Weeks 5–6 | Product Hunt; items 8 and 11; Build replay (item 4) | Second builds per build published |
| Weeks 7–12 | Items 12, 13 and 15; item 14 after a privacy review; Web Push if the owner says yes | Arrivals from search and stores; D30 return rate |

Each week, `node scripts/launch-scorecard.mjs` decides what comes next:
the lowest target with enough data to read is the one to work on.

## Decisions only the project owner can make

1. **Short links for builds kept to their link.** Should the worker store
   them (a privacy change, written up in Privacy.md), or should only builds
   shared to everyone get short links? I recommend the second at first.
2. **Web Push.** Should the rendezvous worker hold push subscriptions and
   the public keys a player follows? It is the strongest retention tool
   available, and the first server-side state tied to a player.
3. **"I'd live here" counts.** Should they be shown, and may Explore sort
   by them? I recommend shown on the build, and not sortable, at first.
4. **Portals that require ads or an SDK.** Accept them, or stay with stores
   and ad-free portals only?
5. **A class space.** Commit to a children's-privacy review before
   offering it to schools?
6. **The launch date.** Every item above is worth less without one.
