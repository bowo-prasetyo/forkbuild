# Teachers and schools

ForkBuild suits a classroom: it runs in any current browser, needs no
accounts or installs, and keeps work on the device. Before recommending it
to a school, say exactly what it does and doesn't do, and don't claim more:
it has not been reviewed against any children's-privacy law (COPPA, GDPR's
rules for children, or a school's own policies), and that review is the
school's to make with the facts below.

**Link:** <https://bowo-prasetyo.github.io/forkbuild/?ref=edu>

## What can be said

All of this is in [Privacy](../Privacy.md):

- No accounts, no email, no names asked for. An identity is a key pair made
  on the device, and the only name is one the student types.
- Builds stay in the browser until the student publishes or shares one.
- The site loads its code only from its own address, sets no cookies, and
  runs no advertising. A once-a-day visitor count (an image from
  GoatCounter) sends a fixed path and nothing about the student; it is off with Global Privacy Control or Do Not
  Track, and can be turned off in **Your Data**.
- Chat and voice need two people to be friends with each other first.

## What a school should know

- **Publishing is public.** A published build, its title, description and
  the student's chosen name can be seen by anyone who gets it, and once
  copied it can't be taken back.
- **Public lobbies reach strangers.** Joining a public lobby (**Peers**, or
  **Lobby** in World View) lists the student's chosen name and lets anyone
  in it connect, which shares their IP address with that person, as any
  peer-to-peer connection does. Students can simply not join one.
- **Work lives in one browser.** Clearing the browser's data, or a shared
  computer wiping it, deletes the builds. **Your Data → Back Up to a File**
  is the only copy.

## A lesson that fits

- Open a ready-made build, change it, and explain what you changed
  (geometry, symmetry, scale).
- The week's build challenge as a short project, entries shared as links
  inside the class only.
- Export a build as STL for the school's 3D printer.
